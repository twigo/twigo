# A part that does not fit gives way in stages, by widths its stages cannot change

Decided 2026-11-15, for the title bar, the details' row of actions, a list's actions and a list editor's columns; on 2026-11-16 for a row of views and a list's scope chip; on 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19 for an object page's header, vitals and layout (ARCHITECTURE 4.1, 4.3, 4.4, 4.9).

## Context

These parts let the browser decide what a narrow width costs them.
The title bar cut the space's name and its namespace scope at their ends while the search field kept its width.
A pod's row of actions was wider than the details, so the actions past their edge could not be seen and still took the Tab key.
Beside the details, the editor's tab went into the overflow's chevron before the list's filters gave anything, and the list switched to a fixed set of columns.

An object's header put a name, an owner, a node and an age on two lines of an inspector as narrow as 320 px, and their parts shrank by CSS.
A name that a cut cannot shorten kept its whole text and painted over the next part, as a crash-looping mock pod's owner and node did at 360 px at `b6823641`, 5.97 px and 9.06 px past the right edge of their links, read as the text's box against its link's with `getBoundingClientRect` (Chrome on the mock, 2026-11-18).

A part measured as it is drawn measures the stage it is at: a fold changes the width that decided it, and the next measure can undo the fold.
A width estimated by counting a text's characters misses for capitals and digits, which are wider than an average letter.

## Decision

- A part that can be too narrow takes the fullest of its stages that fits, in a fixed order kept in one place beside it:
  - the title bar: the search field to an icon, the search out of the middle, the mode chip to its icon, then the crumbs cut in the middle (ARCHITECTURE 4.1);
  - the details' row of actions: the keys, then the labels from the last pinned action back (4.3);
  - a row of views, an object's in the details and in an editor and a side bar's containers (`TabBar`): its trailing tabs into a More menu, one more at each stage, the shown tab never, and past the last fold the shown tab's label cut, down to 2em (4.9);
  - a list's actions, once the tabs have their room: the field's key hint, the Group button's label, the field to an icon, the health control to a menu, then the scope chip, its first name cut in the middle down to its floor, then the chip folded to its glyph and how many more namespaces follow, then to its glyph alone (4.4);
  - a list editor's columns: by each column rule's `drop`, the name column never (4.4);
  - an object's header, whose two lines are facts and not controls (4.3):
    - the first line: the kind's word and the whole name; the name's generated part cut as a list row cuts it; the kind's word gone, its icon staying; the name cut in the middle, down to 12 characters;
    - the second line: every part whole; the node cut in the middle; the owner's name cut too; the owner's kind word gone; the node gone; the namespace cut; the age never cuts and never leaves;
  - an object's vitals and the lines of its containers: a pod's CPU and Memory cell says the share with its base in words, else the base's amount, else the figure alone; the containers' lines of one part say the share with its base in words or without it, one form for every line, the base then in the tooltip; the page filter's field sits beside the views, else on a line of its own, its count in long or short words;
  - the object page itself: the full page from 900 px of the editor's own width, since the editor area can be split, kept down to 884 px, and the inspector's layout under 884 px.
- What decides a stage is never a width the stage changed.
  - The title bar, the row of actions, a row of views, the scope chip, an object's header lines and its vitals' cells measure hidden copies of themselves at their natural size, one per stage, part or form, `aria-hidden` and out of sight in a box of no height.
  - A list's actions read their own row at each stage as it shows, which never shrinks, so that is the stage's natural width, against the room the strip leaves beside its tabs, which the actions do not move.
    The scope chip, which gives way after the row's last stage, is read apart: each stage is read without the chip and counted with the whole chip, and the chip takes the form that fits the room the last stage leaves it, by the widths of its forms from its hidden copies.
    Each of these reads is in a layout effect, and React runs it and the state updates it schedules before the browser paints ([`useLayoutEffect`](https://github.com/reactjs/react.dev/blob/46a54e0592729712b7640308b53268e84fdc7c9f/src/content/reference/react/useLayoutEffect.md?plain=1#L262)), so a turn to a stage that is too wide never shows.
    Beside docked details that room is less what the details take, which is read in a layout effect too as they dock, before the width observers report it.
    A text in the filter changes the counts, and so the row: each count keeps room for the most digits it had with no text, by an invisible copy of them, and while the filter field or its Clear button has the focus no stage moves, so the field never shifts under the caret; nor while the focus is in the scope chip, whose close mark a fold would take away with the focus; while the filter's popover is open the filter stays an icon.
  - A header line's copies draw each stage at its narrowest, a cut text at its floor, so that no two texts share one shortage and the browser never chooses which to squeeze; a text that a cut cannot shorten, a name of 12 characters or fewer, keeps its whole width, and the stage counts it so.
    A copy whose cut has not yet read its font counts as not measured, since it would count a name whole that the cut then shortens.
  - An object page's width reads, `useClientWidth` and `useChildWidths` in `pageStage.ts` (the header's lines, the vitals, the containers' share form and the full page), take an element's width as it attaches, in the render that follows, so that its first paint is at its stage.
    A resize is reported at the start of the next frame, by `requestAnimationFrame(() => flushSync(redraw))`, and never through a store change inside the observer's delivery, which `useClientWidth` did at `b6823641`.
    Such a change renders inside the delivery, takes along the updates that `useWidth` has pending, which resize what lies above the observer, and Chrome raises "ResizeObserver loop completed with undelivered notifications", as it did on a temporary build that reported through a store change, which is in no commit (Chrome on the mock, 2026-11-19); its counts are not kept here.
    A plain update is not rendered inside the delivery: `window.event` is undefined in an observer's callback (Chrome 157.0.8103.80, 2026-11-19), so React gives the update the default priority ([`resolveUpdatePriority`](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/react-dom-bindings/src/client/ReactDOMUpdatePriority.js#L35-L45), React 19.3.1), and runs it as a task of its scheduler ([the priority's level](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/react-reconciler/src/ReactFiberRootScheduler.js#L489-L490), [the scheduler's `MessageChannel`](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/scheduler/src/forks/Scheduler.js#L546-L554)), which comes after the rendering step that delivered the callback and painted ([update the rendering](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/webappapis.html#event-loop-processing-model), read 2026-11-19).
  - The title bar, `ActionRow`, `TabBar`, a list's actions, an embedded list and the page's filter row read their widths with `useWidth` ([`packages/ui/src/lib/useWidth.ts`](../../packages/ui/src/lib/useWidth.ts)).
    It sets its state in the observer's callback, a plain update (above), and a list's actions, a row of views and the page's filter row decide their stages in a layout effect, which runs before the paint of the render that the update causes.
  - The room a list's actions read is the strip's width less every tab at the width it asks for, the 4 px before the first tab, the 2 px between tabs, the overflow button's 28 px and the 6 px after the actions (`useActionsRoom`); beside docked details the least the strip keeps is the space before the tab, a list's tab at its 220 px, the overflow button, the actions at their last stage with the chip as its glyph, and the 6 px after them (`STRIP_KEPT`).
  - A list editor's columns add up the widths in effect, the person's or the rule's, against the grid's measured width.
- Where a width on a boundary could flip a stage or cut a text, the step holds a margin.
  - The row of actions, a row of views, an object's header lines, its vitals' cells, the containers' share form, its filter's row and the full page come back to a fuller stage only with 16 px to spare.
  - A header line holds that margin between its measured reads of one object, and drops it for another object, one with another URI or another `created`, and for a copy whose cut has not read its font: it then takes the fullest stage that fits its new widths, so that the same room gives the same stage whichever object came before.
    An age keeps the room of the widest age of its range (`widestDuration`: `119s`, `9m59s`, `179m`, …), so that its ticking moves no width, and what changes a line's width otherwise, a range of the age included, is held by the margin like a change of room.
  - The title bar counts half a pixel for each rounded width it adds up, so a part stays unfolded only where it fits whichever way the widths were rounded.
  - The scope chip comes back to a fuller form only with a pixel to spare: the room it reads is the row's width less the chip's, two widths each rounded to a pixel, and the form a width was read in changes how they round.
  - A list's actions and a list editor's columns hold none otherwise: neither moves the room it is measured against, so the same room gives the same stage whichever way it came, but for a list's actions while their filter field, its Clear button or the scope chip has the focus, or the filter's popover is open.
- A part folds before it cuts, but for an object's header, whose lines are facts: they cut before they drop.
  The first line's first cut is the name's generated part, as a list row cuts it (`TITLE_STAGES`), and its kind word, which is never cut, leaves before the name is cut in the middle, down to a floor.
  The second line cuts its node and then its owner's name in the middle, drops the owner's kind word and then the node, and cuts the namespace last; its age never cuts and never leaves.
  The title bar cuts its crumbs only once the search, the centring and the chip have given way, and then in the middle, down to a floor of characters with a middle of at least 2em.
  The row of actions only folds; a row of views folds its trailing tabs first and cuts the shown tab's label only where even that and More are too wide; a list's actions fold, and cut only the scope chip's name, in the middle, before they fold it too; and a list editor's columns leave whole, once the name column has given up the free width it takes.
- A part measures once, never once per row: one title bar, one row of actions per details, one row of views per details, editor or side bar, one strip per group, and a grid's own width, from which every row draws its columns; whether a row's hover icons have room comes from the columns' widths.

## Consequences

- What each stage takes is read the same way every time, so the stage is a function of the room, and only the row of actions, a row of views, an object's header lines, its vitals' cells, the containers' share form, its filter's row and the full page, by their margin, the scope chip, by its pixel, and a list's actions, while their filter field, its Clear button or the scope chip has the focus, or the filter's popover is open, depend on the way the room came.
- A width that narrows at once paints one frame at the stage the old width decided in the header's lines, before the next frame's redraw, since the observer reports after layout and the redraw is at the next frame's start.
  That was measured for the header's lines only: Chrome 157.0.8103.80 on the mock at `c1711200`, an Apple M4 Max, 2026-11-19, in both themes, for a crash-looping pod, a pod with a long name and a node, from a per-frame probe of the parts' boxes that is not in the repository, counting for every painted frame the parts of the two header lines outside their line or over each other:
  - a narrowing that arrives at once, the details' width set from 600 to 320 px, 600 to 470, 470 to 320 and 440 to 340 in one run, or the explorer's sash moved at once seven times with an object editor open in a 760 px window, which takes the editor from 320 to 452 px: 3 frames of the run on the crash-looping pod and on the pod with a long name, one at each narrowing that changes a stage, a part outside its line by up to 136 px; none on a node's; none at a widening;
  - the explorer's sash dragged in steps of 4 px in that window: 1 frame on the crash-looping pod and 2 on the pod with a long name, a part outside its line by 3.47 px at most, and none on the node;
  - the details' sash swept from 600 to 320 px and back in steps of 2 px and dragged by the mouse in steps of 4 px, and the window resized from 1,100 to 620 px in steps and then 4 px at a time with an object editor open: no frame in any run;
  - "ResizeObserver loop" errors: none in any of these 36 runs.
  - The vitals, the containers and the full page report as the header's lines do, and were not measured.
  - The parts that read with `useWidth`, the header's `ActionRow`, the views' `TabBar`, the filter row, embedded lists, the title bar and a list's actions, set state in the observer's callback, so their render is a task of React's scheduler after the frame that reported (above): how many frames they paint at the old stage was not measured.
- The hidden copies cost elements: a row of actions draws its pinned actions once more for each stage, a row of views its tabs once more for each fold and the scope chip itself in four forms, for one row per details, editor or side bar and one chip per list, and never for a list's rows; an object's header draws its first line four times and its second six, once per header, and its vitals two copies of a measure's cell.
- The upstream behaviour this rests on:
  - `ResizeObserver` reports an element's content box, without its padding ([content rect](https://www.w3.org/TR/2020/WD-resize-observer-1-20200211/#content-rect-h)), once layout is done and before paint ([event loop](https://www.w3.org/TR/2020/WD-resize-observer-1-20200211/#html-event-loop)); `useWidth` rounds it, and takes a report of 0 for an element that is not laid out, as a tab that dockview detaches, and keeps the width it had.
  - With `text-overflow: ellipsis` the first character on a line is clipped rather than ellipsed ([CSS Overflow 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/2025/WD-css-overflow-3-20251007/#text-overflow)), so a middle narrower than a glyph and the ellipsis shows a letter and no ellipsis, a name that does not exist.
    The middle is a grid track of `minmax(2em, 1fr)`, which never gets narrower than 2em ([`minmax()`](https://www.w3.org/TR/2025/CRD-css-grid-1-20250326/#funcdef-grid-template-columns-minmax)), 26 px at 13 px.
    In the system font at weight 600, the name crumb's, an M and the ellipsis take 23.25 px and `@` and the ellipsis, the widest of M, W, m, w and `@` measured, 23.63 px, so all stay under the floor (Chrome 157 on the mock, 2026-11-15).
  - The title bar's columns are `minmax(0, …fr)`, since an `fr` track alone has an automatic minimum ([`<flex>`](https://www.w3.org/TR/2025/CRD-css-grid-1-20250326/#valdef-grid-template-columns-flex-0)) and would not narrow below the breadcrumb's content.
- Tests stand in for `ResizeObserver`, as `packages/ui/test/resize.ts` and `withScreenSize` in `packages/kube/test/workbench.tsx` do: jsdom lays nothing out, so a test sets the widths and checks the stage, and what is drawn is checked in a browser.
- The terminal's chip in the panel's strip still gives way by CSS alone, its words cut down to its icon (ARCHITECTURE 4.6).
